Why you're not getting interviews

Before you get an interview, your resume usually has to pass some kind of screening. That can be ATS software, recruiter keyword searches, or a fast manual scan.

Resume → Job Description → Match Score → Shortlisted or Ignored

If your resume does not clearly show the skills, keywords, responsibilities, tools, and results the employer is looking for, your application can be overlooked.

How to get more interviews

1. Match your resume to the job description

Do not send the same resume to every job. Compare your resume to the exact job description and make sure your most relevant experience is easy to find.

2. Add missing keywords naturally

Use important words from the job post, but do not stuff keywords. Add them where they truthfully describe your experience, skills, tools, or responsibilities.

3. Make your value clearer

Weak resumes often list tasks. Stronger resumes show what you improved, supported, delivered, handled, built, coordinated, or solved.

4. Improve your match score before applying

A higher match score does not guarantee an interview, but it can help your resume look more relevant to the role before you send it.

Example of a weak resume bullet

Job description: "Project coordination, stakeholder communication, delivery tracking"
Resume: "Helped with projects"

Helped with projects • Assisted team • Worked on tasks

Better version

Coordinated project tasks, tracked delivery progress, and communicated updates to stakeholders.

The stronger version gives clearer signals that the experience matches the job description.
