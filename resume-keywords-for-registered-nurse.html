  • Direct patient care, patient assessment, care planning, medication administration, IV therapy, and vital signs monitoring
  • Unit experience such as acute care, ICU, ER, telemetry, Med-Surg, PACU, pediatrics, oncology, or labor and delivery
  • EHR and EMR systems such as Epic, Cerner, Meditech, clinical documentation, charting, and handoff reports

  • Communication, interdisciplinary collaboration, patient education, discharge planning, and care coordination
  • Clear evidence that you can provide safe patient care, document accurately, communicate with care teams, and follow clinical standards

Example of a weak Registered Nurse resume match

Job description: "Direct patient care, medication administration, IV therapy, EHR documentation, infection control, patient safety"
Resume says: "Worked with patients"

Worked with patients • Assisted team • Helped daily tasks • Supported nurses

This wording is too vague. It does not clearly show direct patient care, medication administration, IV therapy, patient assessment, EHR documentation, infection control, safety protocols, or clinical responsibilities.

Better Registered Nurse resume wording

Provided direct patient care, administered medications and IV therapy, monitored vital signs and patient conditions, maintained accurate Epic EHR documentation, followed infection control protocols, and collaborated with interdisciplinary teams to improve patient safety.

Registered Nurse resume FAQ

What keywords should a Registered Nurse put on a resume?

Registered Nurse resumes should include RN, patient care, medication administration, IV therapy, care planning, vital signs monitoring, triage, wound care, EHR, EMR, clinical documentation, infection control, patient safety, acute care, discharge planning, RN license, BLS, ACLS, and interdisciplinary collaboration when they match your experience.

How do I make my Registered Nurse resume ATS-friendly?

Compare your resume to the job description and add the exact nursing keywords that honestly match your experience. Focus on clinical skills, unit experience, certifications, EHR systems, patient care duties, documentation, patient safety, and measurable care outcomes.

Why is my Registered Nurse resume not getting interviews?

Your resume may be too generic. Phrases like “worked with patients” do not show whether you provided direct patient care, administered medications, used EHR systems, monitored patient conditions, followed infection control protocols, or held required certifications.

Should I include certifications and licenses on my Registered Nurse resume?

Yes. Registered Nurse roles often search for RN license, state nursing license, compact license, BLS, ACLS, PALS, TNCC, NIHSS, CCRN, and other specialty certifications when they match the job requirements.
