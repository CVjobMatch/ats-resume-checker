Typical Occupational Therapist responsibilities

  • Develop individualized occupational therapy plans and functional recovery goals

  • Collaborate with physicians, nurses, physiotherapists, speech therapists, social workers, families, and multidisciplinary care teams

Occupational Therapist resume FAQ

What keywords should an Occupational Therapist put on a resume?

Occupational Therapist resumes should include occupational therapy, OT, ADL, Activities of Daily Living, functional independence, rehabilitation, patient assessment, treatment planning, therapy interventions, cognitive rehabilitation, fine motor skills, upper limb therapy, sensory integration, assistive devices, stroke rehabilitation, neurological rehab, pediatric therapy, elderly care, clinical documentation, and progress evaluation when they match your experience.

How do I make my Occupational Therapist resume ATS-friendly?

Compare your resume to the job description and add the exact Occupational Therapist keywords that honestly match your experience. Focus on ADL training, functional independence, OT interventions, patient groups, rehabilitation techniques, assistive devices, clinical documentation, and measurable therapy outcomes.

Why is my Occupational Therapist resume not getting interviews?

Your resume may be too generic. Phrases like “worked with patients” do not show whether you provided ADL training, improved functional independence, used assistive devices, delivered cognitive rehabilitation, tracked patient progress, or documented OT outcomes.

Should I include patient outcomes on my Occupational Therapist resume?

Yes, if they match your experience. Occupational Therapist roles often value measurable outcomes such as improved ADL performance, increased functional independence, better fine motor control, safer discharge planning, improved mobility, and greater patient participation.
