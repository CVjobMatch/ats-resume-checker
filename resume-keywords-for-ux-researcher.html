UX Researcher resume FAQ

What keywords should a UX Researcher put on a resume?

UX Researcher resumes should include user research, usability testing, user interviews, qualitative research, quantitative research, survey design, A/B testing, journey mapping, persona development, research insights, data analysis, stakeholder communication, Figma, UserTesting, Maze, Hotjar, and user-centered design when they match your experience.

How do I make my UX Researcher resume ATS-friendly?

Compare your resume to the job description and add the exact UX research keywords that honestly match your experience. Focus on research methods, tools, deliverables, data analysis, product collaboration, stakeholder communication, and research impact.

Why is my UX Researcher resume not getting interviews?

Your resume may be too generic. Phrases like “worked on UX” do not show whether you conducted user interviews, ran usability tests, designed surveys, synthesized research, created personas, mapped journeys, or influenced product decisions.

Should I include UX research tools on my resume?

Yes, if you have used them. UX Researcher roles often search for tools such as Figma, UserTesting, Maze, Hotjar, Dovetail, Lookback, Optimal Workshop, Qualtrics, Miro, Mixpanel, Amplitude, and analytics platforms.
