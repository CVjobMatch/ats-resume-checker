Example of a weak nursing resume match

Job description: "Patient care, medication administration, IV therapy, EHR documentation, infection control, BLS, high patient volume"
Resume says: "Worked in hospital"

Worked in hospital • Helped patients • Assisted staff • Supported care

This wording is too vague. It does not clearly show medication administration, IV therapy, patient assessment, EHR documentation, infection control, certifications, patient volume, or specific nursing responsibilities.

Better nursing resume wording

Provided patient care in a high-volume hospital setting, administered medications and IV therapy, monitored vital signs and patient conditions, maintained accurate Epic EHR documentation, followed infection control protocols, and collaborated with multidisciplinary care teams.

The stronger version gives clearer Nurse keywords and shows a better match with the job description.

Typical Nurse responsibilities

  • Provide direct patient care and monitor patient conditions
  • Administer medications, IV therapy, treatments, and clinical procedures safely
  • Monitor vital signs, symptoms, pain levels, patient progress, and changes in condition
  • Maintain accurate EHR documentation, clinical notes, care plans, and handoff reports
  • Follow patient safety, infection control, medication safety, and healthcare protocols
  • Educate patients and families about care plans, medications, discharge instructions, and recovery
  • Collaborate with physicians, nurses, specialists, therapists, and multidisciplinary care teams

Nurse resume FAQ

What keywords should a Nurse put on a resume?

Nurse resumes should include patient care, clinical skills, medication administration, IV therapy, vital signs monitoring, wound care, care planning, patient safety, infection control, patient assessment, clinical documentation, EHR, EMR, Epic, Cerner, RN license, BLS, ACLS, PALS, discharge planning, and multidisciplinary collaboration when they match your experience.

How do I make my Nurse resume ATS-friendly?

Compare your resume to the job description and add the exact nursing keywords that honestly match your experience. Focus on clinical skills, patient care duties, certifications, EHR systems, unit experience, documentation, patient safety, and measurable nursing outcomes.

Why is my Nurse resume not getting interviews?

Your resume may be too generic. Phrases like “worked in hospital” do not show whether you administered medications, provided IV therapy, monitored vital signs, documented care in EHR systems, followed infection control protocols, or handled high patient volume.

Should I include certifications and patient volume on my Nurse resume?

Yes, if they match your experience. Nursing roles often search for RN license, BLS, ACLS, PALS, specialty certifications, high patient volume, patients per shift, EHR systems, clinical procedures, and patient care outcomes.
