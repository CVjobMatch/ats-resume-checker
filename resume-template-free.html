What happens when you apply

Resume Template → Job Description → Keyword Match → ATS Score → Interview

A resume template is only the starting point. What matters most is whether the content inside the template matches the job description. ATS systems and recruiters look for relevant job titles, skills, tools, responsibilities, certifications, and measurable outcomes.

That means the same template can work very well or very poorly depending on how you customize it for each job.

Why most resume templates fail

They use complex formatting

Columns • Graphics • Icons • Text boxes • Tables • Decorative layouts • Hard-to-read fonts

These design elements may look modern, but they can make it harder for ATS software to read your resume correctly.

They focus on design instead of matching

Beautiful layout • Weak keywords • Generic bullet points • No job description alignment

Recruiters care about whether your experience fits the role. A clean template with strong keywords usually performs better than a visual template with weak content.

They encourage generic wording

Hardworking • Team player • Responsible • Helped with tasks • Worked on projects

Generic wording does not show the specific skills, tools, responsibilities, and results that employers are scanning for.

Example of a weak resume template section

Job description: "Project management, stakeholder communication, Jira, reporting, process improvement"
Resume says: "Managed tasks"

Managed tasks • Helped team • Worked on reports

This wording is too vague. It does not show the project management, stakeholder communication, Jira, reporting, or process improvement keywords from the job description.

Better resume template wording

Managed cross-functional project tasks in Jira, coordinated stakeholder communication, prepared weekly reporting dashboards, and improved process tracking to reduce delivery delays.

The stronger version uses clearer keywords and shows how the experience matches the job.

Free resume template FAQ

What is an ATS-friendly resume template?

An ATS-friendly resume template is a simple resume format that applicant tracking systems can read clearly. It uses standard headings, plain text, simple bullet points, and avoids columns, graphics, icons, tables, and complex formatting.

What should a resume template include?

A strong resume template should include contact information, a targeted professional summary, work experience, measurable achievements, relevant skills, tools, certifications, and education. The best version should also include keywords from the job description.

Why do some resume templates fail ATS screening?

Some resume templates fail ATS screening because they use columns, graphics, icons, text boxes, tables, unusual fonts, or complex layouts. These can make it harder for ATS software to read job titles, skills, dates, and experience correctly.

Should I use the same resume template for every job?

You can use the same clean structure, but you should customize the content for each job. Adjust your summary, skills, keywords, and most relevant bullet points so your resume matches the job description.
