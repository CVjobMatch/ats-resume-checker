Is Your Resume Too Generic?

Your resume may be too generic if it could be sent to almost any job without changing much.

Generic resumes often fail because they do not match the job description closely enough. ATS systems and recruiters look for specific job titles, keywords, skills, tools, responsibilities, and measurable results. If your resume sounds broad, your match score may be low even if you are qualified.

What happens when you apply

Generic Resume → Weak Keyword Match → Low ATS Score → Fewer Interviews

A generic resume may look clean, but it often fails to prove fit for a specific role. If the job description asks for Salesforce, customer onboarding, churn reduction, and QBRs, but your resume only says “worked with customers,” the match is weak.

Recruiters are not only looking for general experience. They are looking for evidence that your experience matches their exact role.

Quick self-check

Your resume is likely too generic if:

  • You use the same resume for every job application
  • Your summary does not mention the target role or industry
  • Your skills section looks the same for every job
  • You do not use exact keywords from the job description
  • Your bullet points describe tasks but not outcomes
  • Your experience sounds general instead of role-specific
  • You use phrases like “hardworking,” “team player,” or “responsible” without proof

If this sounds familiar, your resume may be getting filtered out before a recruiter reads it.

Why generic resumes fail

They miss job description keywords

Job description says: SQL, dashboard reporting, stakeholder management, KPI analysis
Resume says: helped with reports

ATS systems often compare the language in your resume with the language in the job description. Missing keywords can make your resume look less relevant.

They use vague skills

Hardworking • Team player • Responsible • Good communication • Fast learner

These phrases are not strong enough by themselves. They do not show tools, responsibilities, achievements, or job-specific experience.

They hide relevant experience

Assisted team with daily tasks

This could mean almost anything. A stronger resume explains what you did, which tools you used, who you helped, and what improved.

Example of a weak generic resume match

Job description: "Customer success, SaaS onboarding, churn reduction, Salesforce, QBRs"
Resume says: "Worked with customers"

Worked with customers • Helped clients • Provided support

This wording is too broad. It does not clearly show SaaS, onboarding, retention, CRM, customer success, or revenue impact.

Better job-matching resume wording

Managed SaaS customer onboarding, tracked customer health in Salesforce, led QBRs, improved product adoption, and reduced churn through targeted retention strategies.

The stronger version is specific. It includes role keywords, tools, responsibilities, and a business outcome.

Generic resume words to replace

Replace vague personal traits

Hardworking • Motivated • Team player • Detail-oriented • Responsible

Use specific proof instead

Reduced reporting turnaround time • Managed customer onboarding • Built Excel dashboards • Improved ticket resolution time • Supported audit documentation

Replace broad tasks

Helped with reports • Assisted managers • Worked on projects • Supported customers

Use role-specific actions instead

Prepared KPI reports • Coordinated stakeholders • Analyzed customer churn • Managed Jira tickets • Built SQL queries • Led onboarding calls

Resume too generic FAQ

How do I know if my resume is too generic?

Your resume may be too generic if you use the same version for every job, rely on vague phrases like “hardworking” or “team player,” miss keywords from the job description, or describe responsibilities without specific tools, skills, achievements, or measurable results.

Why do generic resumes get rejected?

Generic resumes often get rejected because they do not match the job description closely enough. ATS systems and recruiters look for specific job titles, skills, tools, keywords, responsibilities, and outcomes. If those signals are missing, your resume may rank lower.

How do I fix a generic resume?

Compare your resume to the job description, add relevant missing keywords, replace vague skills with specific skills, rewrite broad bullet points into role-specific achievements, and show measurable results where possible.

Should I make a different resume for every job?

You do not need to rewrite everything every time, but you should adjust your summary, skills, keywords, and most relevant bullet points so your resume matches the job description more clearly.
