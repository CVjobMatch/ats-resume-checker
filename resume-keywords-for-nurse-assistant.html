Typical Nurse Assistant and CNA responsibilities

  • Provide hands-on daily patient care in hospitals, nursing homes, long-term care, assisted living, or care facilities
  • Assist patients with bathing, feeding, toileting, dressing, grooming, hygiene, and other activities of daily living
  • Support mobility, ambulation, repositioning, transfers, wheelchair use, and fall prevention
  • Monitor vital signs, observe patient condition changes, and report concerns to nurses
  • Maintain infection control, PPE use, hand hygiene, clean rooms, bed making, and patient safety procedures
  • Document care tasks, patient observations, intake and output, and shift updates accurately
  • Communicate with nurses, patients, families, and healthcare teams to support safe patient care

Nurse Assistant resume FAQ

What keywords should a Nurse Assistant or CNA put on a resume?

Nurse Assistant and CNA resumes should include patient care, CNA, Certified Nursing Assistant, vital signs, ADL support, daily living support, patient hygiene, bathing assistance, feeding assistance, toileting support, mobility assistance, patient transfers, repositioning patients, infection control, documentation, elderly care, long-term care, assisted living, and healthcare support when they match your experience.

How do I make my CNA resume ATS-friendly?

Compare your resume to the job description and add the exact CNA keywords that honestly match your experience. Focus on patient care tasks, ADL duties, vital signs, hygiene care, mobility support, patient transfers, infection control, documentation, care setting, and patient workload.

Why is my Nurse Assistant resume not getting interviews?

Your resume may be too generic. Phrases like “helped patients” do not show whether you assisted with bathing, feeding, toileting, transfers, vital signs, infection control, patient monitoring, or daily care for multiple patients per shift.

Should I include patient volume on my CNA resume?

Yes, if it matches your experience. CNA roles often value measurable workload such as patients per shift, residents supported, beds covered, long-term care workload, hospital ward experience, and high-volume patient care.
