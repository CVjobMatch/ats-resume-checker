If you're applying to jobs and not getting interviews, the problem is rarely your experience.

In most cases, your resume is being filtered out automatically by hiring systems.

The real reason resumes get rejected

Most companies use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to screen resumes.

These systems compare your resume directly to the job description and rank how well it matches.

If your match is too low, your application is rejected instantly.

Top reasons your resume gets rejected

  • Your resume doesn’t match the job description
  • Important keywords are missing
  • Your experience is too generic
  • You’re using the same resume for every job
  • The formatting breaks ATS systems

Example

Job requires: “Data Analysis”
Your resume: “Worked with data”

This looks similar — but to an ATS, it’s not a match.

That difference alone can get your resume rejected.

How to fix your resume

  • Use exact keywords from the job posting
  • Tailor your resume for each role
  • Focus on relevant experience only
  • Show measurable results (numbers, impact)
  • Keep formatting simple and clean

Why most people fail

Most candidates send the same resume to every job.

This guarantees a low match score — and leads to rejection.
