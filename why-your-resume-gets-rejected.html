If you are applying to jobs and not getting interviews, the problem is not always your experience. Often, the resume does not make your fit clear enough for the specific role.

That can happen during ATS screening, recruiter keyword searches, or a fast manual review.

The real reason many resumes get rejected

Many hiring processes compare your resume directly with the job description. Recruiters and ATS systems look for matching skills, tools, job titles, responsibilities, keywords, and evidence that you can do the role.

Resume → Job Description → Match Score → Shortlisted or Rejected

If your resume is too generic or misses important wording from the job description, your application can look weaker than it really is.

Top reasons your resume gets rejected

  • Your resume does not clearly match the job description
  • Important resume keywords are missing
  • Your experience is described with generic wording
  • You are using the same resume for every job
  • Your strongest skills are hidden too low on the page
  • The formatting is harder to scan or parse than it needs to be

Example of a weak match

Job description: “Data analysis, SQL, dashboard reporting”
Resume: “Worked with data”

Worked with data • Helped with reports • Assisted team

This may be true, but it does not clearly match the wording or responsibilities in the job description.

Better version

Analyzed data using SQL, built dashboard reports, and shared insights with the team.

The stronger version gives recruiters and ATS systems clearer signals about the role fit.

How to fix your resume

  • Match your resume to the job description
  • Find missing resume keywords
  • Tailor your resume for each role
  • Focus on relevant experience first
  • Show measurable results when possible
  • Keep formatting simple and clean

Why most people stay stuck

Most candidates send the same resume to every job. That makes the resume easier to reuse, but it often creates a weaker match for each specific role.
