What ATS looks for

Resume → Job Description → Keyword Match → Match Score → Shortlisted or Ignored

ATS systems and recruiters often look for job-specific keywords, tools, skills, certifications, responsibilities, and clean formatting. If your resume is too generic or hard to parse, your application can look weaker than it really is.

Step-by-step: fix your resume for ATS

Step 1: Match your resume to the job description

Use the actual job description as your guide. Look for repeated skills, tools, responsibilities, requirements, and role-specific wording.

Step 2: Add missing keywords naturally

Include relevant tools, skills, job titles, certifications, and responsibilities where they truthfully match your experience.

Use a resume keyword optimizer to find missing terms faster.

Step 3: Simplify formatting

Use a clean, single-column layout. Avoid tables, columns, icons, graphics, and visual skill bars that can make your resume harder to read.

Step 4: Focus on relevant experience

Move the most relevant experience higher on the page and reduce details that do not support the role you are applying for.

Step 5: Improve clarity and proof

Use clear bullet points with specific responsibilities, tools, results, or achievements instead of vague phrases.

Common ATS resume mistakes

  • Missing keywords from the job description
  • Generic wording that could apply to any role
  • Complex formatting, tables, columns, or graphics
  • Important skills hidden too low on the page
  • Sending the same resume to every job
  • Listing skills without showing proof in your experience

Example: weak vs stronger wording

Job description: "Data analysis, SQL, dashboard reporting"
Resume: "Worked with data"

Worked with data • Helped with reports • Assisted team
Analyzed data using SQL, built dashboard reports, and shared insights with the team.

The stronger version is clearer, more specific, and closer to the job description.
