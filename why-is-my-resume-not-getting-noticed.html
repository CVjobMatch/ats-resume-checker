What happens when you apply

Resume → Job Description → ATS Match Score → Seen or Ignored

Before a recruiter notices your resume, it may be scanned by an applicant tracking system. The ATS compares your resume with the job description and looks for relevant job titles, skills, keywords, tools, experience, certifications, and responsibilities.

If your resume match score is too low, your application may never get serious attention. That is why a resume can look good to you but still fail to get interviews.

Main reasons your resume is not getting noticed

Your resume does not match the job description

Job description says: project management, stakeholder communication, Jira, reporting
Resume says: helped with tasks and worked with teams

Recruiters and ATS systems look for clear overlap. If your resume uses different wording from the job post, your experience may be missed.

You are missing important keywords

Missing keywords can include job titles, technical tools, certifications, skills, responsibilities, industry terms, and measurable outcomes.

Use a resume keyword optimizer to find the exact terms missing from your resume.

Your resume is too generic

Worked with customers • Helped projects • Assisted managers • Supported daily tasks

Generic phrases do not show what you actually did. They also make your resume weaker against specific job descriptions.

Your experience is not clearly aligned

A recruiter should not have to guess how your background fits the role.

Your resume should quickly show why your skills, achievements, tools, and responsibilities match the job.

Resume keywords that help you get noticed

Job title and role keywords

Skill and responsibility keywords

Reporting • Stakeholder management • Data analysis • Customer onboarding • Risk assessment • Process improvement • Project delivery • Revenue growth

Tool and system keywords

Excel • SQL • Salesforce • HubSpot • Jira • Power BI • Tableau • Python • Google Analytics • CRM • ATS • ERP

Outcome and achievement keywords

Reduced costs • Increased revenue • Improved retention • Reduced churn • Improved efficiency • Shortened turnaround time • Increased conversion rate

Example of a weak resume match

Job description: "Customer success, SaaS onboarding, churn reduction, Salesforce, QBRs"
Resume says: "Worked with customers"

Worked with customers • Helped clients • Answered questions

This wording is too broad. It does not show the SaaS, onboarding, retention, CRM, or customer success keywords the employer is likely scanning for.

Better resume wording

Managed SaaS customer onboarding, tracked customer health in Salesforce, led QBRs, improved product adoption, and reduced churn through targeted retention strategies.

The stronger version gives recruiters and ATS systems clearer evidence that the resume matches the job description.

Quick resume check

  • Does your resume include the exact job title or a close variation?
  • Does it include the main skills from the job description?
  • Does it mention tools, systems, certifications, or platforms from the job post?
  • Does it show measurable results instead of vague responsibilities?
  • Does your summary match the role you are applying for?
  • Does each bullet point prove fit for the job?

If the answer is no, your resume may be ignored even if you are qualified.

Resume not getting noticed FAQ

Why is my resume not getting noticed?

Your resume may not be getting noticed because it does not match the job description closely enough. Common reasons include missing keywords, weak ATS match score, generic wording, unclear experience, missing job title alignment, and skills that are written differently from the job post.

How do I make my resume more noticeable?

Match your resume to each job description, include the exact skills and keywords from the job post, rewrite generic bullet points, show measurable results, and make your job title, experience, tools, and responsibilities easy to recognize.

Can ATS software ignore my resume?

ATS software can rank your resume lower if it does not contain enough relevant keywords, job titles, skills, tools, or experience from the job description. This can make your resume less likely to be reviewed by a recruiter.

What is the fastest way to fix a resume that is ignored?

The fastest fix is to paste your resume and the job description into a resume match tool. Then add the missing keywords, rewrite vague bullets, improve your summary, and make your most relevant experience easier to see.
