Resume → Job Description → Keyword Match → Shortlisted or Ignored

The biggest mistake most people make

Most resumes are written once and sent everywhere.

That makes applying faster, but it often creates a weaker match for each specific job.

Resumes get stronger when they are tailored to the role.

Step-by-step: how to write a resume that works

1. Start with the job description

Before rewriting your resume, read the job description carefully. Look for repeated skills, tools, certifications, responsibilities, and job title wording.

2. Write a relevant summary

Your summary should show your target role, strongest relevant skills, and the value you bring.

Data analyst with experience in SQL, dashboard reporting, data cleaning, and business insights for operational decision-making.

See more: resume summary examples.

3. Use job-specific keywords naturally

Use important keywords from the job description where they honestly match your experience. Avoid keyword stuffing.

Use the resume keyword optimizer to find missing keywords faster.

4. Show results, not only tasks

Weak bullet points describe activity. Strong bullet points show responsibility, tools, and impact.

Responsible for reports and data tasks.
Built weekly KPI reports in Power BI and improved reporting speed by 30%.

5. Keep formatting simple

Use clear sections, simple headings, and readable formatting. Avoid heavy graphics, icons, tables, and layouts that make the resume harder to scan.

See also: resume format example.

6. Tailor your resume for each job

You do not need to rewrite everything every time. But your summary, skills, and most relevant bullets should match the role you are applying for.

Why many resumes fail

  • They do not clearly match the job description
  • They miss important resume keywords
  • They are too generic for the target role
  • They list tasks without showing results
  • They hide relevant experience too low on the page
  • They use formatting that is harder to scan

Quick resume check

  • Does your resume include the target job title or a close variation?
  • Are your skills written like the job description?
  • Does your experience prove the role fit?
  • Are important tools, systems, or certifications easy to find?
  • Would a recruiter understand your fit in under 10 seconds?

If not, your resume may need clearer keywords, stronger examples, and better alignment with the job.
