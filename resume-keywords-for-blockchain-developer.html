What ATS and recruiters may look for

  • Smart contract development experience using Solidity, Rust, Vyper, Move, or another relevant language
  • Blockchain platforms such as Ethereum, Solana, Polygon, Avalanche, Arbitrum, Optimism, or Base
  • Web3 tools and frameworks such as Hardhat, Foundry, Web3.js, Ethers.js, Wagmi, Viem, OpenZeppelin, Alchemy, or Infura
  • Experience with DeFi, NFTs, DAOs, token development, bridges, or Layer 2 applications
  • Smart contract testing, security review, gas optimization, deployment, and audit readiness
  • Real-world blockchain project experience, GitHub links, deployed contracts, testnet work, mainnet deployments, or protocol contributions
  • Clear evidence that you can build secure, maintainable, and production-ready Web3 systems

Example of a weak Blockchain Developer resume match

This wording is too vague. It does not clearly show smart contract development, Solidity, Ethereum, Web3 tooling, deployment, security, testing, or the specific blockchain tasks recruiters are looking for.

Better Blockchain Developer resume wording

Developed and tested Solidity smart contracts using Hardhat, deployed contracts on Ethereum testnets, integrated DApps with Ethers.js, optimized gas usage, and improved contract security with unit tests and access control checks.

Blockchain Developer resume FAQ

What keywords should a Blockchain Developer put on a resume?

Blockchain Developer resumes should include smart contracts, Solidity, Ethereum, Web3.js, Ethers.js, DApps, DeFi, NFTs, Layer 2, gas optimization, smart contract security, Hardhat, Foundry, Rust, Solana, token development, and blockchain architecture when they match your experience.

How do I make my Blockchain Developer resume ATS-friendly?

Compare your resume to the job description and add the exact blockchain keywords that honestly match your experience. Focus on specific platforms, languages, tools, protocols, testing methods, deployment work, security practices, and real Web3 projects.

Why is my Blockchain Developer resume not getting interviews?

Your resume may be too generic. Phrases like “worked as developer” or “built apps” do not show whether you developed smart contracts, used Solidity, deployed to Ethereum, built DApps, tested contracts, optimized gas, or worked with Web3 tools.

Should I include GitHub or deployed smart contracts on my resume?

Yes. Blockchain hiring teams often want proof of work. Include GitHub links, deployed contracts, testnet deployments, mainnet deployments, audit reports, protocol contributions, or technical case studies when they are relevant and safe to share.
